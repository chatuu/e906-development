% --- Abstract ---
\begin{abstract}
This analysis note details the extraction of absolute Drell-Yan cross sections for $pp$ and $pd$ interactions using data collected with the runs 2 and 3. 
We present these findings for preliminary collaboration approval ahead of upcoming conference presentations. 
This study advances prior analyses by integrating both Liquid Hydrogen (LH$_2$) and Liquid Deuterium (LD$_2$) target datasets, alongside implementing significant methodological upgrades to the efficiency correction framework. 
To mitigate statistical limitations, the reconstruction efficiency is now derived from a global, high-statistics parameterization of the Drift Chamber 1 ($D1$) occupancy, integrated across all kinematic bins (DocDB 11427\footnote{\url{https://seaquest-docdb.fnal.gov/cgi-bin/sso/ShowDocument?docid=11427}}). 
Furthermore, the hodoscope efficiency correction has transitioned from RS67-based paddle efficiency corrections to hodoscope paddle efficiencies averaged over all the roadsets 57-70 in an event-by-event calculation utilizing dimuon track kinematics and paddle-specific response mappings (DocDB 11467\footnote{\url{https://seaquest-docdb.fnal.gov/cgi-bin/sso/ShowDocument?docid=11467}}). 
Building upon these improvements, we report the single-differential cross-sections as a function of transverse momentum, $\diffd\sigma/\diffd p_{T}$, as well as the comprehensive double-differential Drell-Yan cross-sections, $\diffd^{2}\sigma/\diffd x_{F}\diffd M$ and $\diffd^{2}\sigma/\diffd x_{F}\diffd p_{T}$, for both target configurations. 
Finally, we provide comparisons between these extracted distributions and Next-to-Leading-Order (NLO) Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) predictions.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
In October 2025, a preliminary evaluation of the $pp$ Drell-Yan absolute cross section utilizing a 120 GeV proton beam was documented in DocDB 11383\footnote{\url{https://seaquest-docdb.fnal.gov/cgi-bin/sso/ShowDocument?docid=11383}}. The present analysis standardizes the extraction framework for both $pp$ and $pd$ datasets, a necessary harmonization given that the $pp$ cross section is requisite for correcting the hydrogen contamination within the liquid deuterium target.  This analysis incorporates several algorithmic and statistical improvements implemented since the preliminary report:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Global Reconstruction Efficiency Parameterization:} Prior methodologies parameterized reconstruction efficiency as a function of Station-2 occupancy ($D2$), utilizing distinct curves for each kinematic bin. This approach yielded kinematically segregated samples with poor statistical power and consequently large uncertainties. As established in DocDB 11427\footnote{\url{https://seaquest-docdb.fnal.gov/cgi-bin/sso/ShowDocument?docid=11427}}, the reconstruction efficiency exhibits negligible kinematic dependence. Consequently, we now employ a unified, high-statistics efficiency curve parameterized against Station-1 occupancy ($D1$). This shift to $D1$ also eliminates combinatorial ambiguities when computing efficiencies for mixed events.
    \item \textbf{Dimuon-Level Hodoscope Efficiencies:} Earlier studies applied a global hodoscope and trigger efficiency correction (DocDB 10809\footnote{\url{https://seaquest-docdb.fnal.gov/cgi-bin/sso/ShowDocument?docid=10809}}) inherently optimized for $J/\psi$ production. To accurately reflect the Drell-Yan continuum, we have adopted an event-by-event calculation. Utilizing paddle-specific efficiencies derived from single-track data (DocDB 11467\footnote{\url{https://seaquest-docdb.fnal.gov/cgi-bin/sso/ShowDocument?docid=11467}}), the hodoscope efficiency is now computed for each kinematic bin based directly on the constituent tracks of the accepted dimuon pairs.
    \item \textbf{Mixed Event Contamination Correction:} Previously, the reconstruction efficiency correction relied exclusively on the yield of dimuon events in the physical data, neglecting the differing occupancy profiles of mixed events. Because the $D1$ distribution of mixed events diverges from that of regular events, the combinatorial background inherently skewed the efficiency correction. We have addressed this systematic bias by employing a discrete contamination correction method detailed in DocDB 11448\footnote{\url{https://seaquest-docdb.fnal.gov/cgi-bin/sso/ShowDocument?docid=11448}}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Event Selection}
\label{sec:event_selection}
A selection protocol is applied to improve the ratio of pure Drell-Yan events to the underlying continuum of beam-dump interactions and muon combinatorics.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Data Quality:} Only spills explicitly validated by the standard SeaQuest offline quality monitoring are retained. An explicit trigger requirement (\texttt{MATRIX1 == 1}) enforces top/bottom spectrometer coincidence.
    \item \textbf{Track and Vertex Quality:} We mandate the standard collaboration ``Chuck Cuts'' (DocDB 2111\footnote{\url{https://seaquest-docdb.fnal.gov/cgi-bin/sso/ShowDocument?docid=2111}}) to ensure robust spatial reconstruction. This protocol mandates thresholds on track $\chi^2$, hit multiplicity, target-vs-dump vertex probability, and magnet aperture fiducial cuts. The specific boundary conditions are tabulated in \cref{tab:single_track_cuts,tab:dimuon_cuts,tab:occupancy_cuts} of \cref{app:event_selection}.
    \item \textbf{Kinematic Boundaries:} To isolate the Drell-Yan continuum and suppress charmonium resonant states ($J/\psi$, $\psi'$), a lower mass bound of $M_{\mu\mu} > 4.2$ GeV/$c^2$ is strictly enforced, alongside an integration window of $0 < x_F < 0.8$.
\end{itemize}
In addition to these Chuck Cuts we require runID > 11500 for RS62 dataset only, to remove runs where the target position is in question.
We also remove dimuons outside the range of $20 < D1 < 385$ because, the reconstruction efficiency curve is only defined within this range of $D1$ occupancy.

\subsection{Cross-Section Formalism}
The extraction of the double-differential $pp$ cross-section within a given kinematic bin ($\Delta M, \Delta x_F$) follows the formalism outlined in DocDB 11445\footnote{\url{https://seaquest-docdb.fnal.gov/cgi-bin/sso/ShowDocument?docid=11445}}:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\diffd^{2}\sigma_{pp}}{\diffd M \diffd x_{F}} = \frac{1}{\epsilon_{\text{acc}} \mathcal{L} \Delta M \Delta x_{F}} 
    \left[ 
        \frac{Y^{\text{LH2}}_{\text{total}} - Y^{\text{LH2}}_{\text{mixed}}}{\langle\epsilon^{\text{LH2}}_{\text{signal}}\rangle} 
        - \frac{I_{\text{LH2}}}{I_{\text{flask}}} 
        \left( \frac{Y^{\text{flask}}_{\text{total}} - Y^{\text{flask}}_{\text{mixed}}}{\langle\epsilon^{\text{flask}}_{\text{signal}}\rangle} \right)
    \right]
    \label{eq:modified_cross_section}
\end{equation}
where $Y_{\text{total}}$ and $Y_{\text{mixed}}$ denote the normal yields and combinatorial background yields respectively, and $I$ represents the integrated proton flux (POT) incident on a given target. The true signal efficiency, $\langle\epsilon_{\text{signal}}\rangle$, separates the detector response of real correlated pairs from that of mixed tracks:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:avg_signal_eff}
    \langle\epsilon_{\text{signal}}\rangle = \frac{1}{Y_{\text{total}} - Y_{\text{mixed}}}\left[\langle\epsilon_{\text{total}}\rangle Y_{\text{total}} - \langle\epsilon_{\text{mixed}}\rangle Y_{\text{mixed}}\right]
\end{equation}
Here, $\langle\epsilon_{\text{total}}\rangle$ and $\langle\epsilon_{\text{mixed}}\rangle$ are the averaged efficiencies for the normal and mixed yields (DocDB 11448\footnote{\url{https://seaquest-docdb.fnal.gov/cgi-bin/sso/ShowDocument?docid=11448}}) respectively.  The hodoscope and reconstruction efficiencies are uncorrelated, so an efficiency like $\left<\epsilon_{\rm total}\right>$ is the product of the hodoscope and reconstruction efficiences for that collection of events:
\begin{equation}
   \left<\epsilon_{\rm total}\right>=\left<\epsilon_{\rm total,hodo}\right> \left<\epsilon_{\rm total,reco}\right>
\end{equation}
The reconstruction efficiency calculation is described in section~\ref{sec:ktracker_eff} and the hodoscope efficiency calcuation is described in section~\ref{sec:hodo_eff}.

The macroscopic integrated luminosity $\mathcal{L}$ governs the absolute normalization:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = N_{\text{incident}} \cdot \frac{N_{A} \rho L}{A} \cdot f_{\text{atten}}
\label{eq:luminosity}
\end{equation}
where $N_A$ is Avogadro's constant, $A$ is the target molar mass, and $f_{\text{atten}}$ defines the hadronic beam attenuation coefficient. For the $50.8$ cm LH$_2$ flask ($\rho_H=0.0708$~g/cm$^3$), the target areal density is 3.5966 g/cm$^2$ with an associated $f_{\text{atten}} = 0.966$ (DocDB 8588\footnote{\url{https://seaquest-docdb.fnal.gov/cgi-bin/sso/ShowDocument?docid=8588}}). The acceptance parameter $\epsilon_{\text{acc}}$ is derived purely from Monte Carlo sampling.

Extraction of the $pd$ cross section necessitates a subsequent correction for the intrinsic protium (hydrogen) contamination present in the deuterium cryogen:
\begin{eqnarray}
    \frac{\diffd^{2}\sigma_{pd}}{\diffd M \diffd x_{F}} &=& \frac{1}{\epsilon_{\text{acc}} \mathcal{L} \Delta M \Delta x_{F}} 
    \left(
    \left[ 
        \frac{Y^{\text{LD2}}_{\text{total}} - Y^{\text{LD2}}_{\text{mixed}}}{\langle\epsilon^{\text{LD2}}_{\text{signal}}\rangle} 
        - \frac{I_{\text{LD2}}}{I_{\text{flask}}} 
        \left( \frac{Y^{\text{flask}}_{\text{total}} - Y^{\text{flask}}_{\text{mixed}}}{\langle\epsilon^{\text{flask}}_{\text{signal}}\rangle} \right)
    \right] \right. \nonumber \\
    & & \left. - \frac{T_{HD}}{T_{HH}} \frac{I_{\rm LD2}}{I_{\rm LH2}}\left[ 
        \frac{Y^{\text{LH2}}_{\text{total}} - Y^{\text{LH2}}_{\text{mixed}}}{\langle\epsilon^{\text{LH2}}_{\text{signal}}\rangle} 
        - \frac{I_{\text{LH2}}}{I_{\text{flask}}} 
        \left( \frac{Y^{\text{flask}}_{\text{total}} - Y^{\text{flask}}_{\text{mixed}}}{\langle\epsilon^{\text{flask}}_{\text{signal}}\rangle} \right)
    \right]
    \right)
    \label{eq:modified_cross_section_pd}
\end{eqnarray}
We apply the previously measured fractional target thicknesses $T_{HD}= 0.1084$ and $T_{HH}= 3.5966$ (DocDB 11227\footnote{\url{https://seaquest-docdb.fnal.gov/cgi-bin/sso/ShowDocument?docid=11227}}), where $T_{HD}$ is explicitly POT-weighted across the varying purity regimes of the LD$_2$ run periods.

\section{Acceptance Correction}
\label{sec:acc_correction}

The topology of the SeaQuest spectrometer necessitates a correction for its geometric acceptance. The survival probability of a generated Drell-Yan pair is highly dependent on invariant mass ($M$) and longitudinal momentum fraction ($x_F$). The binned acceptance fraction is analytically defined via MC as:
\begin{equation}
\text{Acceptance (A)} = \frac{N_{\text{accepted}}}{N_{\text{thrown}}}
\label{eq:acceptance}
\end{equation}
where $N_{\text{accepted}}$ reflects the yield traversing the digital simulated geometry These are events that satisfy the full reconstruction and selection criteria, including the kinematic cuts. They are extracted from the \texttt{*clean\_occ\_pTxFweight*} MC files, which represent the idealized detector response without background overlays.
(\texttt{*clean\_occ\_pTxFweight*}) and $N_{\text{thrown}}$ denotes the phase space baseline in $4\pi$ (\texttt{*4pi\_pTxFweight*}). To preserve resolution, the array is partitioned into 16 $x_F$ domains (width $\Delta x_F = 0.05$ up to 0.8) and 11 distinct mass bins mapping the high-mass continuum ($4.2 \leq M < 8.7$ GeV/$c^2$).

Visual representations of the acceptance mappings, demonstrating negligible target-dependent geometric variance between LH$_2$ and LD$_2$, are provided in \cref{app:acc_plots}.

\section{Reconstruction Efficiency Correction}
\label{sec:ktracker_eff}
The algorithmic efficiency of the kTracker spatial reconstruction package degrades under the high hit-multiplicity characteristic of the primary beam. To accurately quantify this effect without fragmenting our statistical confidence across narrow kinematic intervals, we derive the primary efficiency metric ($\epsilon_{\text{reco}}$) by comparing "messy" data overlays against idealized "clean" MC tracks.

Following the verification in DocDB 11427\footnote{\url{https://seaquest-docdb.fnal.gov/cgi-bin/sso/ShowDocument?docid=11427}} that $\epsilon_{\text{reco}}$ decouples from the track kinematics, we extract a high-statistics \textbf{global reconstruction efficiency curve} parameterized against the Drift Chamber 1 ($D1$) hit array, as demonstrated in \cref{fig:global_reco_eff}.

    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{./kTrackerEfficiencyPlots/GlobalEfficiencyCurve.pdf}
    \caption{Global Reconstruction Efficiency curve as a function of the $D1$ occupancy variable, integrated over all kinematic bins.}
    \label{fig:global_reco_eff}
\end{figure}

The efficiency for any given dimuon is computed via linear interpolation between the MC-derived curve points ($D1^+, D1^-$):
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon_i = \epsilon(D1^-) + \left(\frac{\epsilon(D1^+) - \epsilon(D1^-)}{D1^+ - D1^-} \right)\left(D1^+ - D1_i\right)
\end{equation}
For combinatorial \textbf{mixed events}, the occupancy is generalized to the arithmetic mean of the parent tracks:
\begin{equation}
    D1_{\text{mixed}} = \frac{D1_{\text{pos}} + D1_{\text{neg}}}{2}
\end{equation}
Since the mixed events have been sorted by the $D1$ value, the three numbers $D1_{\text{mixed}}$, $D1_{\text{pos}}$, and $D1_{\text{neg}}$ are usually identical.

\subsection{Uncertainty Propagation}
For a singular data event $i$, the uncertainty in the interpolated efficiency is:
\begin{equation}
\delta\epsilon_i = \frac{1}{D1^+-D1^-}\sqrt{\left(D1^+-D1_i\right)^2\delta\epsilon(D1^+)^2+\left(D1^--D1_i\right)^2\delta\epsilon(D1^-)^2}
\end{equation}

The efficiency $\langle\epsilon\rangle$ for an $N$-event bin emerges as the simple mean:
\begin{equation}
    \langle\epsilon\rangle = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \epsilon_i \label{eq:avg_eff_2}
\end{equation}
The uncertainties of the individual efficiencies $\epsilon_i$ within a collection of events are correlated because of the use of an interpolation.  The uncertainty in $\langle\epsilon\rangle$ must be calculated taking those correlations into account.  The technique to do that is described in DocDB 11431\footnote{\url{https://seaquest-docdb.fnal.gov/cgi-bin/sso/ShowDocument?docid=11431}}.

We present these efficiencies bin-by-bin for both normal and mixed events, for $(M,x_F)$ bins in \cref{fig:reco_eff_2d},
and similarly for the $p_T$ bins in \cref{fig:reco_eff_pT}.

\section{Determination of Corrected Yields}
\label{sec:corrected_yields}

Determining the signal yield from raw event counts requires scaling by the average signal efficiency $\langle\epsilon_{\text{signal}}\rangle$ defined in \cref{eq:avg_signal_eff}. This extracts the background-subtracted yield for each kinematic bin (see DocDB 11448\footnote{\url{https://seaquest-docdb.fnal.gov/cgi-bin/sso/ShowDocument?docid=11448}}):
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:y_signal}
    Y_{\text{signal}} = \frac{Y_{\text{total}}-Y_{\text{mix}}}{\langle\epsilon_{\text{signal}}\rangle}
\end{equation}
Some special cases:
\begin{itemize}
\item When $Y_{\rm mix}=0$, then $\langle\epsilon_{\text{signal}}\rangle = \langle\epsilon_{\text{total}}\rangle$.
\item When $Y_{\rm total}=0$ and $Y_{\rm mix}=0$, then $Y_{\text{signal}} = 0$. 
\item If $Y_{\rm total}=Y_{\rm mix}\neq 0$ then $Y_{\text{signal}} = 0$.
\end{itemize}

The LH$_2$ corrected yield arises following the dual subtraction of uncorrelated mixed pairs and integrated flask background interactions:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:corrected_yield}
    Y_{\rm corrected}^{\rm LH2}= \frac{Y^{\text{LH2}}_{\text{total}} - Y^{\text{LH2}}_{\text{mixed}}}{\langle\epsilon^{\text{LH2}}_{\text{signal}}\rangle} 
        - \frac{I_{\text{LH2}}}{I_{\text{flask}}} 
        \left( \frac{Y^{\text{flask}}_{\text{total}} - Y^{\text{flask}}_{\text{mixed}}}{\langle\epsilon^{\text{flask}}_{\text{signal}}\rangle} \right)
\end{equation}
The analogous LD$_2$ formalism incorporates the subtraction of the hydrogen contamination:
\begin{eqnarray}
    Y_{\rm corrected}^{\rm LD2}&=& 
        \frac{Y^{\text{LD2}}_{\text{total}} - Y^{\text{LD2}}_{\text{mixed}}}{\langle\epsilon^{\text{LD2}}_{\text{signal}}\rangle} 
        - \frac{I_{\text{LD2}}}{I_{\text{flask}}} 
        \left( \frac{Y^{\text{flask}}_{\text{total}} - Y^{\text{flask}}_{\text{mixed}}}{\langle\epsilon^{\text{flask}}_{\text{signal}}\rangle} \right) \nonumber \\
    & & - \frac{T_{HD}}{T_{HH}} \frac{I_{\rm LD2}}{I_{\rm LH2}}\left[ 
        \frac{Y^{\text{LH2}}_{\text{total}} - Y^{\text{LH2}}_{\text{mixed}}}{\langle\epsilon^{\text{LH2}}_{\text{signal}}\rangle} 
        - \frac{I_{\text{LH2}}}{I_{\text{flask}}} 
        \left( \frac{Y^{\text{flask}}_{\text{total}} - Y^{\text{flask}}_{\text{mixed}}}{\langle\epsilon^{\text{flask}}_{\text{signal}}\rangle} \right)
    \right]
    \label{eq:y_corrected_ld2}
\end{eqnarray}

\section{Uncertainty Calculation}
\label{sec:error_calc}

Our uncertainties contain both statistical and systematic contributions. 
Statistical uncertainties arise from quantities like $Y_{\rm total}$ and $Y_{\rm mix}$, while systematic uncertainties arise from corrections like $\langle\epsilon_{\rm signal}\rangle$.
Starting with \cref{eq:corrected_yield}, we propagate the uncertainties using the usual techniques.

\subsection*{Statistical Uncertainty Components}

\begin{align}
\frac{\partial Y_{\rm corrected}}{\partial Y^{\textrm{LH2}}_{\textrm{total}} } &= \frac{1}{\langle\epsilon^{\textrm{LH2}}_{\textrm{signal}}\rangle} \\
\frac{\partial Y_{\rm corrected}}{\partial Y^{\textrm{LH2}}_{\textrm{mixed}} } &= -\frac{1}{\langle\epsilon^{\textrm{LH2}}_{\textrm{signal}}\rangle} \\
\frac{\partial Y_{\rm corrected}}{\partial Y^{\textrm{flask}}_{\textrm{total}} } &= - \frac{I_{\textrm{LH2}}}{I_{\textrm{flask}}} \frac{1}{\langle\epsilon^{\textrm{flask}}_{\textrm{signal}}\rangle} \\
\frac{\partial Y_{\rm corrected}}{\partial Y^{\textrm{flask}}_{\textrm{mixed}} } &= \frac{I_{\textrm{LH2}}}{I_{\textrm{flask}}} \frac{1}{\langle\epsilon^{\textrm{flask}}_{\textrm{signal}}\rangle}
\end{align}

\subsection*{Systematic Uncertainty Components from Efficiencies}

\begin{align}
\frac{\partial Y_{\rm corrected}}{\partial \langle\epsilon^{\textrm{LH2}}_{\textrm{signal}}\rangle} &= -\frac{Y^{\textrm{LH2}}_{\textrm{total}} - Y^{\textrm{LH2}}_{\textrm{mixed}}}{\langle\epsilon^{\textrm{LH2}}_{\textrm{signal}}\rangle^2} \\
\frac{\partial Y_{\rm corrected}}{\partial \langle\epsilon^{\textrm{flask}}_{\textrm{signal}}\rangle} &= \frac{I_{\textrm{LH2}}}{I_{\textrm{flask}}} \frac{Y^{\textrm{flask}}_{\textrm{total}} - Y^{\textrm{flask}}_{\textrm{mixed}}}{\langle\epsilon^{\textrm{flask}}_{\textrm{signal}}\rangle^2}
\end{align}

\subsection*{Uncertainty Propagation}
Assuming Poisson variance for yields ($\sigma^2_Y = Y$), we add statistical uncertainties in quadrature.

\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\delta Y^{\rm stat}_{\rm corrected} = 
\Biggl[ & \left( \frac{\partial Y_{\rm corrected}}{\partial Y^{\textrm{LH2}}_{\textrm{total}} } \right)^2 Y^{\textrm{LH2}}_{\textrm{total}} 
+ \left( \frac{\partial Y_{\rm corrected}}{\partial Y^{\textrm{LH2}}_{\textrm{mix}} } \right)^2 Y^{\textrm{LH2}}_{\textrm{mix}} \\
& + \left( \frac{\partial Y_{\rm corrected}}{\partial Y^{\textrm{flask}}_{\textrm{total}} } \right)^2 Y^{\textrm{flask}}_{\textrm{total}} 
+ \left( \frac{\partial Y_{\rm corrected}}{\partial Y^{\textrm{flask}}_{\textrm{mix}} } \right)^2 Y^{\textrm{flask}}_{\textrm{mix}} \Biggr]^{1/2}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
By construction, the fractional statistical uncertainty holds for the cross section:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\delta \sigma^{\rm stat}}{\sigma} = \frac{\delta Y^{\rm stat}_{\rm corrected}}{Y_{\rm corrected}}
\end{equation}
The systematic uncertainty from the efficiency correction is:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\delta Y^{\rm sys, eff}_{\rm corrected} = 
\Biggl[ & \left( \frac{\partial Y_{\rm corrected}}{\partial \langle\epsilon^{\textrm{LH2}}_{\textrm{signal}}\rangle} \, \delta \langle\epsilon^{\textrm{LH2}}_{\textrm{signal}}\rangle \right)^2 \\
& + \left( \frac{\partial Y_{\rm corrected}}{\partial \langle\epsilon^{\textrm{flask}}_{\textrm{signal}}\rangle} \, \delta \langle\epsilon^{\textrm{flask}}_{\textrm{signal}}\rangle \right)^2 \Biggr]^{1/2}
\end{split}
\end{equation}

The ultimate systematic uncertainty $\delta\sigma^{\rm syst.}$ represents the sum of three primary contributions:
 \begin{itemize}
 \item Monte Carlo spatial acceptance correction ($\mathcal{A}$) [\cref{sec:acc_correction}]
 \item Efficiency corrections [Detailed above]
 \item Invariant mass contamination via the $\psi'$ high-mass tail ($Y_{\psi'}/Y_{DY}$) [\cref{app:psi_prime_study}]
 \end{itemize}
Each of those contributions are evaluated as follows:
\begin{align}
\delta\sigma_{\rm acc}^{\rm syst.} &= \left( \frac{\delta \mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{A}} \right) \sigma \\
\delta\sigma_{\rm yield}^{\rm syst.} &= \left( \frac{\delta Y^{\rm sys, eff}_{\rm corrected}}{Y_{\rm corrected}} \right) \sigma \\
\delta\sigma_{\psi'}^{\rm syst.} &= (Y_{\psi'}/Y_{DY}) \times \sigma
\end{align}
and then are combined in quadrature:
\begin{equation}
\delta\sigma^{\rm syst.} = \sqrt{ \left(\delta\sigma_{\rm acc}^{\rm syst.}\right)^2 + \left(\delta\sigma_{\rm yield}^{\rm syst.}\right)^2 + \left(\delta\sigma_{\psi'}^{\rm syst.}\right)^2 }
\end{equation}
A similar method is used in the case of the LD$_2$ yields and cross section.

\section{Appendix: Event Selection Criteria (Chuck Cuts)}
\label{app:event_selection}

These cuts suppress upstream and downstream background interactions as well as combinatorics. 

In the subsequent tables, the $y$-axis displacement generated by the beam geometry defines the intrinsic offset $y_{\text{beam}} = 1.6$ cm.

\subsection{Single Track Cuts}
Table~\ref{tab:single_track_cuts} lists discrete geometric and analytic cuts govern individual muon trajectories.

\begin{table}[!ht]
    \centering
    \caption{Single track selection criteria.}
    \label{tab:single_track_cuts}
    \begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Variable} & \textbf{Condition} \\ 
        \midrule
        \textbf{Track Fit Quality} & \\
        Target $\chi^2$ & $\chi^2_{\text{target}} < 15$ \\
        Reduced $\chi^2$ & $\chi^2 / (N_{\text{hits}} - 5) < 12$ \\
        Vertex Assumption & $\chi^2_{\text{target}} < 1.5 \times \chi^2_{\text{dump}}$ AND $\chi^2_{\text{target}} < 1.5 \times \chi^2_{\text{upstream}}$ \\
        \midrule
        \textbf{Hits \& Geometry} & \\
        Number of Hits & $N_{\text{hits}} > 13$ \\
        Station 1 Z-Momentum & $9 < p_{z, \text{st1}} < 75$ GeV/$c$ \\
        Single Track Vertex $z$ & $-320 < z_{v} < -5$ cm \\
        \midrule
        \textbf{Aperture \& Trajectory} & \\
        Radial pos. at target ($z \approx -130$ cm) & $x^2 + (y-y_{\text{beam}})^2 < 320$ cm$^2$ \\
        Radial pos. at dump ($z \approx 50$ cm) & $16 < x^2 + (y-y_{\text{beam}})^2 < 1100$ cm$^2$ \\
        Vertical Focusing & $y_{\text{st1}} / y_{\text{st3}} < 1$ \\
        Vertical Projection & $y_{\text{st1}} \cdot y_{\text{st3}} > 0$ \\
        Min Vertical Momentum & $|p_{y, \text{st1}}| > 0.02$ GeV/$c$ \\
        \midrule
        \textbf{Momentum Conservation} & \\
        KMag Momentum Kick & $||p_{x, \text{st1}} - p_{x, \text{st3}}| - 0.416| < 0.008$ GeV/$c$ \\
        Vertical Bend (Null) & $|p_{y, \text{st1}} - p_{y, \text{st3}}| < 0.008$ GeV/$c$ \\
        Longitudinal (Null) & $|p_{z, \text{st1}} - p_{z, \text{st3}}| < 0.08$ GeV/$c$ \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Dimuon Cuts}
Table~\ref{tab:dimuon_cuts} lists cuts made when combining positive and negative tracks to form dimuons.

\begin{table}[!ht]
    \centering
    \caption{Dimuon kinematic and vertex selection criteria.}
    \label{tab:dimuon_cuts}
    \begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Variable} & \textbf{Condition} \\ 
        \midrule
        \textbf{Vertex Position} & \\
        Transverse Offset ($x$) & $|dx| < 0.25$ cm \\
        Vertical Offset ($y$) & $|dy - y_{\text{beam}}| < 0.22$ cm \\
        Radial Vertex & $dx^2 + (dy - y_{\text{beam}})^2 < 0.06$ cm$^2$ \\
        Longitudinal Vertex ($z$) & $-280 < dz < -5$ cm \\
        Vertex Fit Quality & $\chi^2_{\text{dimuon}} < 18$ \\
        Vertex Consistency & $|\chi^2_{\text{trk1}} + \chi^2_{\text{trk2}} - \chi^2_{\text{dimuon}}| < 2$ \\
        \midrule
        \textbf{Kinematics} & \\
        Invariant Mass & $4.2 < M_{\mu\mu} < 8.8$ GeV/$c^2$ \\
        Feynman-$x$ & $-0.1 < x_F < 0.95$ \\
        Transverse Scaling $x_T$ & $0.05 < x_T \le 0.58$ \\
        Costh (Collins-Soper) & $|\cos \theta| < 0.5$ \\
        Longitudinal Momentum & $38 < p_z < 116$ GeV/$c$ \\
        Transverse Momentum limits & $|dp_x| < 1.8$ GeV/$c$, $|dp_y| < 2.0$ GeV/$c$ \\
        Total $p_T$ & $dp_x^2 + dp_y^2 < 5.0$ (GeV/$c$)$^2$ \\
        Track Separation & $\text{sep} < 270$ cm \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Occupancy and Topology Cuts}
Table~\ref{tab:occupancy_cuts} lists occupancy and other hit selections made within the kTracker routine.

\begin{table}[!ht]
    \centering
    \caption{Occupancy and topological cuts.}
    \label{tab:occupancy_cuts}
    \begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Variable} & \textbf{Condition} \\ 
        \midrule
        \textbf{Chamber Occupancy} & \\
        Drift Chamber 1 Hits & $D1 < 400$ \\
        Drift Chamber 2 Hits & $D2 < 400$ \\
        Drift Chamber 3 Hits & $D3 < 400$ \\
        Total Chamber Hits & $D1 + D2 + D3 < 1000$ \\
        \midrule
        \textbf{Topology} & \\
        Opposite Quadrants & $y_{\text{st3}}^{\text{trk1}} \cdot y_{\text{st3}}^{\text{trk2}} < 0$ \\
        Total Hits on Tracks & $N_{\text{hits}}^{\text{trk1}} + N_{\text{hits}}^{\text{trk2}} > 29$ \\
        Station 1 Hits Sum & $N_{\text{hits, st1}}^{\text{trk1}} + N_{\text{hits, st1}}^{\text{trk2}} > 8$ \\
        Station 1 X-Sum & $|x_{\text{st1}}^{\text{trk1}} + x_{\text{st1}}^{\text{trk2}}| < 42$ cm \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}
